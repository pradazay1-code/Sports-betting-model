## 3. MODEL & METHOD `[MODEL]`

Week 1 is one game. Per-play metrics do not stabilize for 4–6 games, so 2026 data is
regressed hard toward a 2025 prior:

**Weights: 65% 2025 full season · 25% 2026 Week 1 (regulation only) · 10% league mean.**

League mean = **22.9 PPG**, derived rather than assumed: Buffalo ranks **15th of 32** in
points allowed at 22.9, so the 15th-ranked team is essentially the league median, and
league points scored ≡ league points allowed.

Regressed rates:

| | Offense | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| DET | 26.88 | 24.09 |
| BUF | 30.07 | 24.92 |

Neutral-field expectation uses the **additive** form, `(X_off + Y_def) / 2`.
I tried the multiplicative form (`X_off × Y_def / L`) first and rejected it: with both
offenses above average and both defenses below, it compounds and produced a **61-point**
neutral total, which is absurd. Additive gives **DET 25.90 / BUF 27.08, total 52.98,
BUF -1.18** — landing on top of the market total, which is the right kind of agreement.

**Adjustments applied to the neutral number:**

| Adjustment | Size | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DET offensive line | **-2.0 DET** | RT Blake Miller + G Christian Mahogany both OUT. Fresh news, and OL injuries are systematically underpriced. |
| DET secondary | **+1.5 BUF** | Branch (PUP), Joseph, Arnold (released), Reed (Q). Sized **down** from ~3.5 because this is stale news the market absorbed weeks ago, and the 25% Week-1 component was *already played without Branch and Joseph*. |
| Short week (TNF) | **-0.75 each** | Symmetric; hits the total, not the side. `[READ]` |
| Home field | **+2.0 BUF**, split ±1.0 | Standard modern-NFL HFA. No bonus added for the stadium opening — the crowd case is real but not quantifiable, so it sits as upside risk rather than a number. |

**Buffalo's coaching change:** Joe Brady is the new head coach `[FACT, verified]`, promoted
from OC. That is the *highest-continuity* coaching change there is, so I did **not** shift
Buffalo's offensive prior — only widened its uncertainty. McDermott's departure is a real
downgrade risk to the defense, and Week 1's 31 points allowed already moved that number,
so I did not shift it a second time.

**Final projection: DET 22.15 · BUF 28.83 · total 50.98 · BUF -6.68.**

**Reproduce every number in §4 with one command:**

```
python3 -m lib.simulate --home BUF --away DET \
  --home-points 28.83 --away-points 22.15 --home-td-share 0.64 \
  --spread -4.5 --total 53.5 --half -n 60000 --seed 20260917
```

**Simulation.** Drive-level Monte Carlo (`lib/simulate.py`), **60,000 iterations**, scores
built from actual 3s and 7s so key-number mass is reproduced rather than smoothed away by
a normal draw. Teams are correlated through a shared pace factor and a shared scoring-
environment factor.

*Variance calibration:* tuned on a neutral pick'em to margin SD **14.25** / total SD **15.6**.
Both are slightly **wider** than the real NFL (~13.5 each). Wide is the conservative
direction here — it lowers the favorite's cover probability and shrinks my stated edge.
Tightening to the true SD would make every recommendation below look *better*, not worse.

*Known model limitation:* the sim puts **7.3%** of margins on 3, against an empirical
**9–10%**. It has no end-of-half or trailing-team field-goal logic. This understates the
value of buying through the 3 — relevant to the -3/-3.5 rows in the table, irrelevant at
-4.5 where I am actually betting.

**Where the market is probably right, and I am probably wrong `[READ]`:**

a multiway market: convert each offered price to a Poisson scoring rate

touchdown count.

### 7d. The check that kills most prop "edges" `[MODEL]`

A yardage line is set near the **median**, not the mean. Yardage distributions are
right-skewed, so median < mean. Projecting a mean and comparing it straight to the line
manufactures a fake edge on every right-skewed market. Backing the implied mean out of each
line first:

| Prop | Line | cv | Line implies a mean of | My mean | Gap | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gibbs rushing | 90.5 | 0.42 | 98.2 | 96.5 | -1.7 | noise — **no bet** |
| Cook rushing | 78.5 | 0.45 | 86.1 | 83.0 | -3.1 | noise — **no bet** |
| D.J. Moore receiving | 58.5 | 0.55 | 66.8 | 64.5 | -2.3 | noise — **no bet** |
| Allen rushing | 30.5 | 0.62 | 35.9 | 33.0 | -2.9 | noise — **no bet** |
| **Goff passing** | 270.5 | 0.28 | 280.9 | **258.0** | **-22.9** | **real** |
| **Kincaid receiving** | 49.5 | 0.62 | 58.2 | **64.8** | **+6.6** | **real** |

This is also what killed the Allen rushing under, which looked like +28% before the
correction and is a **pure artifact of the variance assumption** after it. His line at 30.5
implies a mean of 35.9 against his retrieved 2025 average of 34.1. That is a book pricing a
skewed distribution correctly, not an error.
